# Product Idea — Snudly

## One-liner
Snudly gives Scandinavian parents—starting with first-time Norwegian dads—one clear answer about what their baby should wear for the weather and activity.

## Background
Sivert is a golf professional, a parent, and a hobbyist vibe coder who uses AI-assisted development rather than a traditional software-engineering background. The idea comes from repeatedly debating whether his own children needed headwear or more warmth around the torso and wanting a faster, calmer answer.

## The problem
Parents of babies aged 0–24 months repeatedly ask, “What should the baby wear right now?” Temperature alone is insufficient because wind, rain, age, activity, stroller or carrier use, sleep, and car-seat safety change the answer. Today, parents typically ask a partner or caregiver, receive conflicting opinions, and only learn afterward—by checking the baby's neck or chest—whether the choice was right.

## Target user
The initial early adopter is a first-time Norwegian dad entering his baby's first cold or wet season, who sometimes dresses the child independently for stroller walks, daycare, errands, or outdoor sleep. Norway is the launch market; Sweden and Denmark are the next localized markets, while mothers, co-parents, grandparents, and caregivers remain secondary users.

## Proposed solution
Snudly is a mobile app that combines Nordic weather data with the baby's age, current activity, and non-overridable safety rules. The user chooses the child and activity, then receives a numbered outfit in dressing order with short explanations and suitable substitutions. The magic moment is when a dad taps “Finn dagens antrekk” and the recurring family debate ends within seconds because everyone has one concrete answer.

## Why you
Sivert experiences the problem with his own children and understands what a tired parent needs in the decision moment; his background as a golf professional also trains him to turn complex inputs into simple, actionable guidance.

## Risky assumptions
- Parents will pay repeatedly for Snudly despite free advice and free core recommendations from competing apps.
- Infant-specific activities, country-specific safety rules, and dad-first positioning create a clear preference for Snudly over existing alternatives.
- Localized source attribution and external review earn enough trust in Norway, Sweden, and Denmark without Snudly presenting its guidance as medical advice.
