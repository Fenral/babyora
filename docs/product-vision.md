# Product Vision — Snudly

## 1. Vision & Mission

### Vision Statement

Scandinavian parents step outside with their babies feeling prepared rather than uncertain, because safe clothing decisions no longer depend on guesswork or household debate.

### Mission Statement

Snudly turns local weather, the baby's age, and the planned activity into one clear, safety-bounded outfit recommendation that a tired parent can act on within seconds.

### Founder's Why

Sivert is building Snudly because this is not an abstract parenting problem. With his own children, the same questions return whenever the weather changes: Does the baby need a hat? Is the torso warm enough? Is this too much clothing for the stroller, carrier, or car seat? The available answer is usually another opinion, followed by checking the child's neck or chest after the decision has already been made.

His professional background as a golf professional matters more than it first appears. Coaching is the work of translating several interacting variables into one practical next action without overwhelming the person receiving the advice. Sivert is not trying to make parents into weather experts. He is using AI-assisted development to build the calm decision aid he wanted as a parent: one answer, a short reason, and clear safety boundaries.

### Core Values

1. **Give one answer, not another dashboard.** Snudly may process temperature, wind, precipitation, age, and activity, but the parent should receive a concrete outfit in dressing order. Extra data is shown only when it helps the decision.

2. **Safety outranks preference and conversion.** A manual garment swap, a growth target, or a subscription gate must never bypass a hard safety rule. When a recommendation reaches the boundary of reliable guidance, Snudly says so plainly and asks the parent to check the child.

3. **Explain enough to earn trust.** Every meaningful recommendation should include a short reason in ordinary language and make its source traceable. Snudly avoids unexplained authority as carefully as it avoids clinical overload.

4. **Earn each country separately.** Norway, Sweden, and Denmark share climate patterns but not identical guidance, language, or parenting norms. Snudly launches Norway first and treats Swedish and Danish expansion as fresh validation work, not translation work.

5. **Measure behavior, not compliments.** A parent saying the idea is useful is not validation. Repeated recommendations, successful real-weather use, and a genuine payment decision are the evidence that determines what Snudly builds next.

### Strategic Pillars

1. **Own the infant decision.** The defensible position is not generic weather clothing. It is the 0–24-month decision across stroller, carrier, outdoor play, indoor sleep, and car-seat contexts, with age-aware safety boundaries.

2. **Deliver the magic moment before asking for commitment.** The first real outfit recommendation must appear before the paywall. A parent should understand the value from experience, not from marketing copy.

3. **Launch narrow, learn quickly.** The first public-quality experience is Norwegian and aimed at first-time dads entering a baby's first cold or wet season. Swedish and Danish pilots follow only after the core recommendation and payment model show evidence.

4. **Build trust as a product capability.** Country-reviewed rules, visible source context, graceful uncertainty, privacy restraint, and external scenario review are part of the product—not launch paperwork added later.

### Success Looks Like

In 12 months, Snudly has a stable iOS and Android product in Norway, Sweden, and Denmark, with every country's safety-sensitive recommendation set externally reviewed. At least 25,000 families use it monthly, 5,000 pay, and the product sustains a trial-to-paid conversion rate above 20% without hiding the first recommendation behind a paywall. Parents describe Snudly as “the answer we check before going out,” and partners or caregivers can reproduce the same recommendation without reopening the original debate. If the 14-day validation shows strong use but weak payment, success instead means the company has changed the value model early—keeping today's answer free and charging for planning, multiple children, and family coordination—rather than scaling an unproven subscription premise.

## 2. User Research

### Primary Persona

**Martin, 28–38, first-time Norwegian dad.** Martin has a baby under 12 months and is entering the child's first cold or wet season. He is comfortable with mobile apps but is not interested in interpreting weather models, TOG tables, or long parenting articles. His relevant routine is intensely practical: get the child ready for daycare, a stroller walk, errands, or a visit while managing bags, time pressure, and incomplete sleep.

Today, Martin checks the temperature, asks his partner what she thinks, and sometimes adds or removes a layer based on instinct. The conversation feels disproportionate to the decision because neither parent is completely certain and the consequences—an uncomfortable, overheated, or cold baby—feel personal. He will switch when Snudly produces a more specific answer than a weather app, shows why it chose the layers, respects real infant safety constraints, and does so faster than asking someone else.

Martin does not want to be “educated” during the departure. He wants to feel capable and calm. If Snudly appears alarmist, asks for an account too early, or returns a recommendation that contradicts obvious local practice without explanation, trust is lost immediately.

### Secondary Personas

**Nora, co-parent and trust calibrator.** Nora already has strong opinions about the baby's clothing and may be the person Martin currently asks. She values Snudly when it makes the reasoning visible and produces consistent answers across caregivers; she rejects it if it positions itself as more authoritative than qualified health guidance.

**Kari, grandparent or occasional caregiver.** Kari does not use Snudly daily but wants the same current recommendation the parents see. Her tolerance for setup is low, and her highest-value outcome is avoiding a call or message for every outing.

**Alex, daycare worker or nanny.** Alex handles several children and changing weather. Snudly is relevant only if recommendations are fast, clearly tied to one child and activity, and framed as guidance rather than an instruction that conflicts with institutional routines.

### Jobs To Be Done

**Functional jobs**

- When I am about to take my baby outside, tell me exactly which layers to use for this child, weather, and activity so I can leave without researching.
- When someone else is caring for my child, let them see the same recommendation so we do not make separate guesses.
- When conditions or activities change, show what changes in the outfit rather than forcing me to reconstruct the whole decision.
- When a suggested garment is unavailable, offer a safe equivalent without silently weakening a safety-critical layer.

**Emotional jobs**

- Help me feel prepared rather than negligent or uncertain.
- Reduce the tension of conflicting parental opinions without declaring one parent “wrong.”
- Reassure me that the recommendation has boundaries and will admit uncertainty.

**Social jobs**

- Help me be seen as a capable, involved dad who can make the decision independently.
- Give caregivers a shared reference point that makes coordination feel calm and competent.

### Pain Points

| Rank | Pain | Frequency | Current response | Consequence |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conflicting opinions when the parent wants one answer | Several times per week in changeable weather | Ask a partner or caregiver and negotiate the layers | Moderate emotional friction and delayed departures; occasionally the wrong outfit |
| 2 | Weather data does not translate into infant clothing | Daily during active use | Mentally combine temperature, wind, rain, and activity | Repeated cognitive load; highest for first-time parents |
| 3 | The parent learns the result too late | After an outing or sleep period | Feel the baby's neck, chest, hands, or feet | The child may already be sweaty, cold, or uncomfortable |
| 4 | Different activities need different warmth | Every change between stroller, carrier, play, sleep, or car | Apply a general layering rule inconsistently | Safety and comfort risk, especially for stationary infants and car seats |
| 5 | Caregivers cannot reproduce the decision | Whenever care changes hands | Send messages or explain the outfit manually | Coordination cost, but lower urgency than the immediate recommendation |

The pain is real, but its direct financial cost is low. That is the central business blind spot: frequent use does not automatically imply subscription willingness.

### Current Alternatives & Competitive Landscape

| Alternative | What it does well | Where it falls short | Switching requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask a partner, grandparent, or daycare worker | Free, familiar, and socially trusted | Produces conflicting opinions and depends on the other person being available | Snudly must answer faster and feel at least as trustworthy |
| Weather app plus general layering guidance | Accurate conditions and no new product to learn | Leaves the parent to translate weather into baby-specific layers and activities | Snudly must hide the calculation while explaining the decision |
| Check the baby's neck or chest afterward | Direct feedback from the child | Reactive rather than preventive and easy to interpret inconsistently | Snudly must still encourage physical checks while improving the starting point |
| Kledd | Nordic weather-based layers, a free daily answer, planning, and notifications | Focuses on kindergarten life rather than the 0–24-month activity and safety decision | Snudly must make the infant specialization unmistakable before charging |
| BabyDress, BabyWeather, and similar apps | Demonstrate that parents understand the category and may pay for premium features | Significant overlap in outdoor, sleep, profile, forecast, and wardrobe features | Snudly must win on localized safety evidence, activity contexts, dressing-order clarity, and dad-first positioning |

The real enemy is not a competitor download. It is the household habit of asking someone else for free. Direct competitors make the subscription model harder, but the habitual alternative defines the speed and trust bar.

### Key Assumptions to Validate

1. **We assume first-time dads experience enough decision friction to adopt a dedicated app.** Validate by observing 15 recent clothing decisions and recruiting at least 5 dads who install and use Snudly in the same week.

2. **We assume repeated use can support a subscription.** Validate with a real payment decision after seven days; stated willingness does not count. The initial threshold is at least 5 payment commitments from 20 testers.

3. **We assume infant-specific activity modes are a meaningful differentiator.** Validate by having parents use Snudly and a competitor, then identify the reason for their preference without prompting. At least 7 of 10 should cite the infant/activity/safety experience.

4. **We assume one recommendation can be clear without appearing falsely precise.** Validate by testing comprehension and confidence across 30 real weather scenarios, including boundary cases where the app must express uncertainty.

5. **We assume country-specific sourcing earns trust.** Validate safety scenarios with qualified reviewers in Norway, Sweden, and Denmark and separately test whether parents understand that the product is guidance, not medical advice.

6. **We assume dad-first positioning attracts dads without alienating other caregivers.** Validate acquisition and activation by segment; dads should over-index in the initial cohort while co-parents still rate the voice as inclusive.

7. **We assume MET Norway data and the product's derived rules are operationally reliable enough for daily use.** Validate stale, missing, extreme, and rapidly changing weather states on real devices before public release.

## 3. Product Strategy

### Product Principles

1. **The result is the product.** Weather visualization, mascots, and tools support the experience but cannot delay or obscure the outfit recommendation.

2. **One-handed and sleep-deprived is the default context.** Primary actions must remain obvious, touch targets forgiving, copy short, and critical states usable outdoors, in low light, and with reduced attention.

3. **Safety rules are deterministic and testable.** Generative output must not decide safety-critical clothing. Every hard rule is versioned, sourced, scenario-tested, and applied again after a manual substitution.

4. **Uncertainty is shown, not hidden.** When weather is stale, location is unavailable, or a threshold is close, Snudly states the limitation and gives a conservative next action rather than manufacturing confidence.

5. **No account before value.** The core recommendation and first magic moment work locally. Authentication appears only when the parent chooses a shared capability that genuinely needs it.

6. **Country expansion is a product release.** Language, sources, thresholds, store copy, review evidence, and real-family testing ship together per country.

### Market Differentiation

Snudly competes in a category where “weather in, outfit out” already exists. Its defensible position is narrower and more demanding: an infant-specific decision engine for 0–24 months, covering how warmth changes across stroller, carrier, outdoor play, indoor sleep, and car-seat contexts. The product shows layers in the order a parent puts them on, explains the reason in ordinary language, and gives non-overridable safety rules a separate authority from convenience preferences.

That distinction matters because the target parent does not need a prettier weather card. He needs the discussion to end. Country-reviewed evidence, a calm dad-first voice, local Scandinavian language, and reliable behavior at the dangerous edges make the recommendation more useful than a generic temperature lookup. The moat is not the raw weather source—competitors can use MET Norway too. It is the reviewed rule system, scenario evidence, trust earned through repeated accurate use, and the product discipline to stay focused on the infant decision.

### Magic Moment Design

The magic moment is: **Martin taps “Finn dagens antrekk” and, within seconds, sees one numbered outfit for this baby, activity, and weather, plus a short reason he can repeat to another caregiver.**

For this to happen reliably:

- Onboarding asks only for inputs required by the engine: age or birth date, home location, and consent for relevant device capabilities.
- The current weather is fetched or served from a clearly labeled recent cache before calculation.
- Activity selection is visible and understandable without opening a secondary tool.
- The deterministic engine returns a complete, conflict-free set of layers and applies safety overrides before rendering.
- The result appears in dressing order, distinguishes required from conditional items, and explains the largest weather or safety driver.
- The first real recommendation appears before a paywall or account requirement.
- Failure states preserve a usable cached recommendation when safe and make staleness explicit.

The shortest path is onboarding → activity confirmation → one tap → outfit. The target is under 60 seconds for a first-time user and under 5 seconds for a returning user with current inputs. Anything that delays this path—including family setup, wardrobe cataloging, or extended education—stays outside the validation MVP.

### MVP Definition

The MVP is a 4–8-week launch-hardening package built from the useful parts of the existing app, followed by the 14-day behavioral test. The current app is a functional prototype and evidence source—not a visual baseline. Proven logic, integrations, and flows should be reused where they help. Preserve the approved four-tab information architecture as the starting structure; screens, colors, typography, component styling, imagery, and page layout remain open for evidence-led redesign.

1. **Local child and location setup.** Capture one child's birth date or age band and one home location without requiring an account. Done means the parent can complete setup one-handed, understand why each input is needed, and edit it later.

2. **Four-context recommendation flow.** Use live or clearly cached MET Norway weather with age and activity inputs for stroller, carrier, outdoor play, and indoor sleep. Done means every supported scenario produces either a complete recommendation or a bounded, actionable error state.

3. **Deterministic safety layer.** Apply non-overridable rules for headwear, sleep, blankets, car-seat clothing, stroller covering, and extreme conditions. Done means every rule has a source, version, automated tests, and external-review status.

4. **Numbered outfit result.** Present garment images and names in dressing order with role, short rationale, and safe substitution where available. Done means a tester can explain what to put on without opening another screen.

5. **Trial and real payment decision.** Show the first real recommendation before the seven-day trial/paywall sequence and support monthly and annual RevenueCat packages. Done means purchase, restore, expiry, and cancellation guidance work on a physical iPhone in sandbox and production-review configurations.

6. **Norwegian public-quality localization plus controlled Swedish and Danish pilots.** Norwegian is release-ready; Swedish and Danish copy and safety scenarios are explicitly marked pilot until reviewed. Done means no untranslated user-facing string appears in the tested flow.

7. **Behavioral measurement and crash visibility.** PostHog records privacy-safe funnel events, and Sentry captures scrubbed production errors. Done means the team can measure activation, recommendation use, trial start, payment decision, and failures without sending child names, birth dates, precise coordinates, or recommendation details.

### Explicitly Out of Scope

1. **Public family sharing and caregiver accounts.** Valuable for retention, but auth, RLS, invitations, and shared-child consent do not test whether the core answer is useful or paid. Reconsider after the Norway payment test passes.

2. **Multiple children in the validation MVP.** This is a natural premium feature but increases profile and entitlement complexity before the core value is proven. Reconsider in the first post-validation release.

3. **Full week planning and afternoon change alerts.** Competitors already use planning as paid differentiation, but it should not obscure the test of today's recommendation. Reconsider if the core answer is used but subscription conversion is weak.

4. **Wardrobe registration, photo analysis, generic AI chat, social feeds, and affiliate shopping.** These add friction, privacy risk, or commercial incentives that conflict with a calm decision aid. They remain out of scope through the first year unless the strategy changes explicitly.

5. **Personal calibration beyond safety boundaries.** Useful after repeated feedback, but it needs evidence and careful limits. Reconsider only after the base engine passes external review and enough outcome feedback exists.

6. **Public Android and full Sweden/Denmark release.** The shared Capacitor codebase remains compatible, but the initial quality gate is Norwegian iOS. Reconsider after real-device payment and recommendation validation; Swedish and Danish controlled pilots may run earlier.

7. **Widgets, morning notifications, and extended tools.** These can improve habit formation but do not prove the initial magic moment. Reconsider after the two-week cohort shows repeat use.

### Core User Flows

**Flow 1 — First recommendation**

1. Trigger: Martin installs Snudly after a referral or dad-focused video.
2. He selects Norwegian, adds the baby's birth date, and confirms home location or enters a place manually.
3. Snudly explains that recommendations are guidance and asks only for needed permissions.
4. Martin chooses stroller, carrier, outdoor play, or indoor sleep.
5. He taps “Finn dagens antrekk.”
6. Snudly shows the numbered layers, rationale, source context, and any hard warning.
7. Outcome: Martin can dress the baby without asking another person.
8. Success: completed in under 60 seconds with no account and no paywall before the result.

**Flow 2 — Returning decision in changed weather**

1. Trigger: Martin opens Snudly before another outing.
2. The app loads the saved child and location, labels weather freshness, and shows the current activity.
3. If the same input fingerprint is already calculated and fresh, Snudly shows the cached result immediately; otherwise it recalculates.
4. Martin changes the activity or substitutes a garment.
5. Safety rules run again and the result updates.
6. Outcome: the answer remains fast without hiding changes or weakening safety.
7. Success: result visible within 5 seconds on a normal connection or immediately from a safe fresh cache.

**Flow 3 — Trial and payment decision**

1. Trigger: Martin has experienced the real recommendation and reaches the trial/paywall moment.
2. The paywall describes monthly and annual plans using live store prices and clearly states the seven-day trial and renewal behavior.
3. Martin starts a trial or declines; the app preserves the truthful free/paid boundary.
4. On purchase, RevenueCat updates the entitlement and the app unlocks the relevant access without restart.
5. On restore, cancellation, expiry, or network failure, the app explains the state and offers recovery.
6. Success: physical-device purchase and restore pass; funnel events record the outcome without PII.

### Success Metrics

**Primary metric: paid activation.** A household counts as paid activated when it completes at least four recommendation sessions in seven days and then starts or commits to a paid plan.

| Metric | Good | Great | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 14-day testers requesting recommendations on 4+ days | 12/20 | 16/20 | Demonstrates repeat behavior rather than novelty |
| Genuine payment commitments after the test | 5/20 | 8/20 | Tests the core business assumption |
| Trial-to-paid conversion after launch | 15% | 20%+ | Connects the seven-day experience to revenue |
| First-use magic moment completion | 70% | 85%+ | Shows onboarding does not block value |

The 90-day business target remains 500 active families, 100 paying subscribers, and 20% trial-to-paid conversion, but it should not be treated as permission to scale if scenario review or payment evidence fails.

## 4. Brand Strategy

### Positioning Statement

For first-time Scandinavian parents—starting with Norwegian dads—who need to know what their baby should wear right now, Snudly is the infant clothing decision companion that turns local weather, age, and activity into one clear outfit. Unlike generic weather tools and temperature-based outfit apps, Snudly accounts for how babies regulate warmth across stroller, carrier, play, sleep, and car-seat contexts, with country-reviewed safety rules that preference cannot override.

### Brand Personality

Snudly is the calm, practical friend who has checked the conditions, understands the baby is not a small adult, and gives you the answer without making you feel foolish for asking. This person speaks in short, sensory sentences, wears simple functional clothing, and is comfortable saying “check the neck again in a few minutes” when the situation cannot be reduced to certainty.

Snudly is quietly confident, never superior. It does not dramatize normal weather, flood the screen with warnings, or congratulate the parent for basic care. It never uses fear to sell a subscription, never turns a safety caveat into legal sludge, and never pretends a mascot is proof of the recommended outfit.

### Voice & Tone Guide

The voice is always calm, concrete, warm, and concise. Tone becomes more direct when safety is involved, more encouraging during onboarding, and more matter-of-fact during errors. It never becomes clinical or cute at the expense of clarity.

| Context | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding | “Legg inn fødselsdatoen, så tilpasser vi lagene til alderen.” | “For å optimalisere den personaliserte termoreguleringsprofilen må du fullføre registreringen.” |
| Error state | “Vi fikk ikke hentet været akkurat nå. Prøv igjen, eller bruk sist oppdaterte råd fra kl. 08.10.” | “Oops! Noe gikk galt 😅” |
| Empty state | “Velg hva dere skal gjøre, så finner vi lagene.” | “Her var det tomt! Kom i gang ved å opprette din første aktivitet.” |
| Success message | “Dagens antrekk er klart — fire lag, innerst først.” | “Fantastisk! Du er en superpappa!” |
| Safety warning | “Dropp den tykke dressen i bilstolen. Selen skal ligge tett mot kroppen.” | “ADVARSEL: Feil bekledning kan være livsfarlig.” |
| Marketing copy | “Slutt å diskutere luen. Få ett klart svar før dere går ut.” | “Revolusjonerende AI gir perfekte klær for alle babyer.” |
| Payment | “Prøv i 7 dager. Deretter fornyes abonnementet til prisen som vises her.” | “Fortsett gratis” when the action actually starts a renewing trial |

### Messaging Framework

**Tagline:** Ett klart svar før dere går ut.

**Homepage headline:** Hva skal babyen ha på seg i dag?

**Homepage support line:** Snudly bruker været, alderen og det dere skal gjøre til å foreslå lagene—innerst først.

**Value propositions**

1. **One decision, not more data.** Snudly translates the conditions into the actual layers instead of asking the parent to interpret another forecast.
2. **Built around babies and activities.** A baby in a stroller, carrier, car seat, or indoor sleep setting does not need the same clothing.
3. **Safety with visible boundaries.** Hard rules cannot be switched off, recommendations show their reason, and the app remains explicit that guidance does not replace checking the child.

**Feature descriptions**

- **Dagens antrekk:** A numbered list in the order the clothes go on, tailored to the child, activity, and current weather.
- **Trygghetsregler:** Non-overridable checks for sleep, car seats, stroller covering, headwear, blankets, and extreme conditions.
- **Bytt plagg:** Safe alternatives when the suggested item is unavailable, followed by a fresh safety check.
- **Planlegg:** Future weather changes expressed as clothing changes, not a repeated forecast. Deferred until the core paid-value test is complete.

**Objection handlers**

- “I can just ask my partner.” — You can. Snudly is for the moments when you want the same quick answer without restarting the discussion.
- “Every baby is different.” — Yes. Snudly gives a safer starting point based on age, weather, and activity, then reminds you to check your own child.
- “Is this medical advice?” — No. Snudly is practical clothing guidance with explicit safety boundaries and sources; it does not diagnose or replace health professionals.
- “Why pay when weather apps are free?” — Weather apps show conditions. Snudly does the baby-specific translation and maintains the safety and activity logic. The payment test must prove that difference is worth recurring payment.

### Elevator Pitches

**5 seconds**

Snudly tells you what your baby should wear today.

**30 seconds**

Parents check the weather and still debate whether the baby needs a hat, another torso layer, or less clothing in the carrier. Snudly combines local weather with the baby's age and activity, then gives one numbered outfit with clear safety boundaries. It is designed first for Norwegian dads who want an answer, not another forecast.

**2 minutes**

Every parent of a baby has had the same conversation: What should the baby wear right now? The temperature is only one input. Wind, rain, age, whether the child lies still in a stroller or shares body heat in a carrier, and whether the next step is a car seat or sleep all change the answer. Most parents solve this by asking someone else and checking the child afterward.

Snudly turns those inputs into one concrete outfit in dressing order. The system uses live Nordic weather, deterministic infant clothing logic, and non-overridable rules for safety-sensitive situations. The parent sees the answer first and a short reason second—without an account or paywall blocking the initial value.

We are starting narrowly with first-time Norwegian dads entering their baby's first cold or wet season. The app already exists, so the immediate work is not speculative feature building; it is a 14-day behavioral test with 20 dads across Norway, Sweden, and Denmark, including a genuine payment decision. If repeated use is strong but payment is weak, we will keep the daily answer free and monetize planning, multiple children, and family coordination. The ask is simple: help us put the real flow in front of parents and qualified country reviewers, then let their behavior determine the expansion.

### Competitive Differentiation Narrative

Most products in this category either stop at “feels like” temperature or add a generic clothing lookup to a weather forecast. Kledd shows that a Nordic daily recommendation can be offered free and monetized through planning and multiple children; BabyDress and BabyWeather show that outdoor, sleep, profile, and wardrobe features are already available. Snudly therefore cannot win by claiming to be the first weather-based baby outfit app. It wins only if parents experience it as the most trustworthy infant decision: 0–24-month logic, activity-specific warmth, hard safety overrides, country-reviewed sources, and a numbered answer that ends the conversation. The strategy is to prove that narrow advantage with Norwegian dads before spending to look Scandinavian.

## 5. Visual Design

Visual direction is intentionally open. The active neutral contract is
[`docs/design-exploration.md`](design-exploration.md): it asks how a tired
first-time dad can understand and trust one outfit within five seconds, then
defines the functional, safety, trust, and accessibility boundaries that every
option must satisfy. It deliberately selects no color, typography, component
character, imagery, density, theme, or design system.

The existing app, root `DESIGN.md`, CSS, mockups, assets, and design-lab
artifacts are reference material only. None is the control direction or locks
the product to a current visual choice because it already exists. Before
production styling, use external references to create at least three materially
different directions, test the same onboarding/home/result scenario with five
target dads, and record only the evidence-supported **provisional** direction
in `docs/design.md`. Treat that document as versioned guidance that may evolve
after user testing—not as a permanent constraint.
